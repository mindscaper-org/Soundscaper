CorridorKey has a proprietary license.

It reads: 

> This work is licensed under the Creative Commons
Attribution-NonCommercial-ShareAlike 4.0 International Public License
(CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), the full text of which is included below, subject
to the following additional terms and conditions. These additional
terms supplement the CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 licence and take precedence
where they conflict.

The terms then go ahead to essentially do a full "all rights reserved", prohibiting distribution, among other things. However, it then also goes on to require modified versions to be distributed free of charge. 

After some consideration, we have decided to go with the most likely interpretation of the following section of the readme instead: 

> Use CorridorKey for whatever you'd like, including processing footage for commercial projects. What you make with it is yours.
>
> *   You may not sell this tool, or charge for access to its code or model, and you may not offer it as a paid API or inference service, unless given permission by Corridor Digital.
> *   You may build on the code and model, but what you release must be freely accessible, stay under this same license, and keep the CorridorKey name. Donationware and pay-what-you-want are fine.

Our interpretation is that inclusion into Framescaper and other Mindscaper projects is fine as long as we keep it free.

In a strict open source sense, this makes Framescaper partially proprietary, however, as the model isn't bundled with Framescaper *anyway* (all machine-learning stuff is only downloaded on-demand), we don't see a strong reason to exclude it. Unless, of course, the rights holders have a different idea in mind.
